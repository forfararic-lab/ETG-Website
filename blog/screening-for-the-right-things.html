The Honest Assessment August 2026

Are We Screening for the Right Things?

Your screening process measures stability. The role demands adaptability.

You know the problem. You just don't know how to fix it.

"Five years experience in operations management" used to mean something. It was a filter that worked well enough. It screened out obviously unprepared people. It gave you a baseline. It was quantifiable, defensible, checkbox complete.

In 2026, it doesn't seem to mean much.

Why the Filter Broke

The filter didn't break because it was a bad filter. It broke because the jobs changed faster than hiring did.

Five years ago, an operations manager's role was relatively stable. The ERP system was established. The processes were documented. The plant layout was fixed. Training a new hire meant teaching them how your specific place worked.

Now? Reshoring is pulling manufacturing back to the U.S. Facilities are being built from scratch with technology that didn't exist three years ago. Platforms are being replaced. Supply chains are reorganizing. The role that existed in 2020 is not the role that exists in 2026.

Don't even get me started on what AI is doing to companies right now.

A person with "five years operations experience" learned to operate within a stable environment. But they may be unprepared for an environment that changes annually.

That's not their fault. It's your filter's fault.

The Cost of Staying With the Filter

You post the job with the credential requirements you've always used. Your ATS screens for keywords. You get resumes that look right on paper. You interview people who check all the boxes.

And six months in, you realize they can't do the job.

Not because they lied. Because the job changed between the time you wrote the description and the time they started. Or the job requires skills that only exist in people who've adapted to constant change, not people who've mastered a fixed system.

So you look for a new hire. You write the same description. You get the same results.

The filter doesn't break once. It breaks repeatedly. And every time, you tell yourself the candidate pool is just getting worse.

The candidate pool isn't worse. Your filter is.

What Matters in 2026

The role you need to fill is hard to put on paper. It exists but it's different than it was a year ago, and it could be different a year from now.

You can't write a job description for that. You can't screen for that on a resume. Your ATS will never find it.

What you need is someone who:

Learns fast enough to master systems before the next change hits. Doesn't panic when the tools they know become obsolete. Sees a broken process and figures out how to fix it. Understands your industry deeply enough to know which rules matter and which ones are legacy.

You can't quantify those things. You can't reduce them to years of experience or certifications. And most hiring processes have no mechanism to measure them.

So you're trapped. You need something unmeasurable. Your process only accepts measurable things. So you pick the closest measurable proxy and pretend it's the same.

It's not.

The Uncomfortable Truth

Most companies are not in a position to solve this because it requires an understanding of how to translate the skills that matter, not just check marks in a screening process. It requires talking to hiring managers about what they need, not what they think they should ask for. It requires being honest about whether the role is ready to fill or whether you need to do other work first.

Companies are drowning in resumes. But their filters are designed to find the wrong things. So they screen for five years of experience and wonder why the hire doesn't work out. More volume doesn't fix a broken filter.

That takes time. It slows down hiring. It makes things harder before it makes them easier.

The companies that figure out how to hire for adaptability instead of credentials will lean into the next five years. They'll thrive. Everyone else will keep posting the same job description and wondering why the results don't change.

The Question That Matters

"Five years experience in X skill" isn't a solution for 2026 because the skills you need don't have five years of history behind them yet.

The question isn't "how do we find people with the right credentials?" It's "how do we hire for people who can figure it out?"

That's a different conversation entirely. It requires different questions, different interviews, different ways of evaluating fit.

It's harder. But it's a conversation worth having.

If you are trying to figure out whether a gap on your leadership bench is a hiring problem or something else, we would rather spend 20 minutes understanding the situation than send you a stack of resumes that will not work out.
