Candidate Truth September 2026

The Fastest Cleared Hire Is the One Who Already Has a Clearance

A cleared role does not fill on offer day. It fills on adjudication day, and most hiring plans do not account for the gap in between.

A cleared role does not fill on offer day. It fills the day the clearance comes through, and right now that day is four to nine months out for a candidate starting from zero, even at the fast end of current DCSA reporting.

Most hiring plans do not account for that gap. They get built as if signing an offer is the finish line.

It is not. A candidate without an existing clearance is a candidate with a four to nine month lead time attached, whether the hiring manager plans for it or not. Programs do not wait quietly for nine months. Milestones slip. Someone else absorbs the workload. By the time the new hire finally clears, the role they were hired for may not look like the one that got posted.

Think about clearance status the way a plant manager thinks about a supply chain. A company sourcing candidates without regard to clearance status is sourcing out of a supply chain with a four to nine month lead time on every single unit. It does not matter how good the sourcing is upstream of that. The lead time is the constraint.

There is a pool of talent that skips it. Roughly two hundred thousand service members separate from the military every year. A meaningful share of them walk out holding an active clearance, or one recent enough to reinstate in a fraction of the time a new investigation takes. That is not a talent story about discipline or mission focus. It is inventory that is already stocked, sitting in a supply chain most companies are not sourcing from.

Most contractors are not set up to reach that population before it scatters. A transitioning service member leaves the base, takes the first offer in front of them, or spends a year in a job that has nothing to do with the work they are qualified for, long before anyone in a cleared program thinks to look for them.

Here is where it gets more complicated than hire a veteran with a clearance and call it solved. Not every clearance transfers cleanly. One that lapsed past the reciprocity window has to be reinvestigated close to scratch, which erases the speed advantage entirely. A candidate cleared at a lower tier than the role requires does not fix a Top Secret timeline.

The honest version of this search accounts for both variables at once. Right tier, right recency, right operational background, in that order, and a straight answer when a candidate checks one box but not the other. Sometimes that means telling a client the fastest available candidate is not fast for their specific program. That answer costs a placement in the short term. It is still the right one.

The programs that hit their staffing timelines this year will not be the ones posting the fastest. They will be the ones who worked out months ago which candidates were already carrying the inventory everyone else is waiting on DCSA to deliver.

If you are trying to figure out what a candidate's military background actually means for the role you are filling, we would rather spend 20 minutes understanding the situation than send you a stack of resumes that will not work out.
