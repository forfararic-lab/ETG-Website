Hiring August 2026

You Adopted Skills Based Hiring. Now What?

Credentials matter. But they're only part of the answer.

You hired someone last quarter with an outstanding industry record. Eight years with a competitor. Perfect background for the role. On paper, they had exactly what you needed. Three months in, it's clear they don't have what the role requires.

Not the skills. Not the experience. The orientation.

The Hire That Skills Based Hiring Was Supposed to Prevent

This candidate can manage operations. They can run a system. They know the industry cold. But they're built to maintain, not build. They're happy to run out the clock. The role needs someone hungry to grow, to challenge the status quo, to iterate the process until it's better than it was yesterday.

That's not a skills gap. That's an orientation gap.

And here's what makes this painful: You bought skills based hiring specifically to go deeper than credentials. Not to ignore them. To ask what comes after.

Eight years in the industry is a real signal. It matters. But it was enough confidence that you stopped asking the next question. And that question is the one that determines whether they're the right person or just the safe person.

Why Skills Based Hiring Misses the Orientation Problem

Skills based hiring defines competency as what you can do. Manage a budget. Run operations. Lead a team. Solve technical problems. Those things are measurable. You can screen for them.

But orientation, how you think about the work, is almost never part of the skills taxonomy.

Are you the kind of leader who keeps things running, or the kind who keeps things evolving? Do you see the current playbook as the solution or the starting point? When something breaks, do you fix it the way it was before, or do you ask if there's a better way?

That gap between someone who can do the job and someone who will drive the job forward is enormous. And skills based hiring doesn't screen for it.

So you end up with candidates who look great on the skills checklist. They have experience. They know the systems. They can execute the playbook. But they lack the hunger or the thinking style the role needs.

How Credentials Stopped You From Asking the Harder Question

This happens because skills based hiring gave you permission to stop thinking past credentials. The system handles the screening, right? Define the skills, run the candidates through the framework, and the right person surfaces.

Except: when someone has eight years of direct industry experience, your brain doesn't turn off. You see the credential and nod. The skills framework confirms what the credential already suggested.

Your TA leader showed you candidates. You made the hire. And credentials were enough confidence that you didn't ask the next question.

The Real Problem Skills Based Hiring Created

The system promised to separate what you can do from who you are. But it only handles the first half.

It defines competency. It doesn't define character. It doesn't measure whether someone is building or maintaining. It doesn't surface whether someone sees the current state as the goal or as the foundation.

And without that, you can hire someone with perfect skills and zero orientation for what the role requires.

This matters more than most people think, because operational leadership roles aren't stable. They're changing. AI is moving. Margin is tightening. Supply chains are fragmenting. The person you need isn't someone who can run today's playbook better.

It's someone who will question the playbook and rebuild it when it stops working.

Skills based hiring doesn't ask that question. Your TA leader probably should have. But the credentials were so loud that nobody asked it. And three months later you're frustrated.

What This Means For Your Next Hire

Credentials matter. Eight years in your industry is a real signal. It gets you 70% of the way there.

But that last 30% is orientation. Is this person built to maintain what's working, or to question it and rebuild it?

That's the question your TA leader should surface. That's the question you should answer before you make the hire.

Until you do, credentials will keep feeling like enough. And you'll keep hiring the safe person instead of the right person.

If you are standing up a new facility or filling technical roles that have stayed open longer than they should, we would rather spend 20 minutes understanding the situation than send you a stack of resumes that will not work out.
