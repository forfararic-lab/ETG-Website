Honest Assessment August 2026

The Leader about to Leave

A plant manager sees what needs to happen. Internal TA knows the process is broken. But the process is approved. And that gap is the problem.

You can see it in the room

The plant manager is three months into a critical backfill and the team is burning out covering the gap. He calls TA and says: I need this filled ASAP. The problem is that TA is managing more open requisitions with less people, and it's beginning to show. Intake meetings are being skipped, screenings are missing important information and the process is suffering because of it. TA wants to help, but they know that this requisition will spend twelve to fourteen weeks in motion. Not because anyone decided twelve to fourteen weeks was ideal. Because that is how long everything takes now. Job requisition approval. Posted to external channels. Screening process with triple the application volume and half the time per candidate. Internal review cycles. Then we start talking to candidates. Everyone knows the process. They also know that it does not work anymore because everyone is drowning.

ASAP is not impatience

ASAP is not impatience. It is math. If he begins to lose his team to other companies his productivity goes down, revenue goes down, and he increases the workload of those that remain. So, the plant manager digs in, and spends valuable time running a parallel search. LinkedIn. Calls to his network. Conversations with people he knows. He is not going around TA. He is going around a system that is overloaded.

Everyone is caught in a different market

TA sees this. TA knows the process is broken. TA has probably said so in a meeting. In an all hands. In a one on one with their leader. TA has probably been told: This is the process. These are the controls. We need consistency. We need compliance. We need to prove our value through metrics. TA is buried under the contradiction. Prove your value. Maintain hiring velocity. Do it with less headcount. Do it while managing double the application volume and forty four day time to fill. TA is not wrong about needing structure. The plant manager is not wrong about needing speed. But they are now operating in two different realities at the same time.

This is when you start to lose him

The plant manager fills the role through his network in seven weeks. TA never submits a candidate. The requisition closes. The plant manager has proven something to himself: the organization's process is not the constraint. The organization is. This is when you start to lose him. It is not dramatic. He does not resign in anger. He stops asking permission. He stops believing the system will work for him when it matters. He stops investing in change because change requires believing the organization will move. He starts keeping one eye on the door.

The system became the constraint

TA is caught in the middle of something they did not create. They know the process is broken. They followed it anyway because that is what they are supposed to do. They did what they were told. They did it right. And it still did not work. Because the work itself became impossible somewhere around requisition number eighteen. The process protects you from fast mistakes. It also protects you from fast wins. It was designed for a market that did not move this quickly. The people who approved it five years ago were not wrong. The market was different then. It is not different anymore. But the process is still approved. The headcount is still frozen. TA is still drowning. Changing it means risk. It means going to the people who built the controls and saying: This does not work. It means admitting that the controls were built for a different pace. It means politics. The plant manager is not interested in politics. He has a plant to run and production numbers to hit. He has people deciding whether to stay.

The organization moved at 40 mph. The market moved at 100.

So he waits. He complies. He runs parallel searches. He fills roles through his network. And one day, he takes a job somewhere else. Somewhere that moves at the speed he needs to move so he does not need to take on additional work to get things accomplished. You will be surprised when he leaves. You will also wonder why your best leaders keep going to work for your competitors. The answer is not that your leaders are impatient. It is not that TA is incompetent. It is that your organization stayed the same while the market got faster. And TA became the mirror. Everyone sees it. No one can fix it. And the people who could move either move themselves or move to a place that can move.

If you are trying to figure out whether a gap on your leadership bench is a hiring problem or something else, we would rather spend 20 minutes understanding the situation than send you a stack of resumes that will not work out.
